# Raw Thoughts: Freshman Onboarding

## What would make ME skeptical as a freshman?

- "35 Tbits/sec" - sounds like marketing BS
- "Shape IS compute" - sounds like philosophy wank
- Dense theory before I see anything work
- Being told what to think instead of discovering it
- Files that explain instead of demonstrate
- No way to break things and see what happens

## What hooked me when I was learning?

- Running something and it WORKED
- Changing one thing and seeing the effect
- "Wait... that shouldn't work... but it does?"
- Building something I could show someone
- Puzzles, not lectures
- Feeling clever when I figured something out myself

## What's wrong with the current 10 files?

Looking at them honestly:
- 01_wait_what.py - Good hook, but ends with a lecture
- 02_so_what.py - Too much explaining
- 03_build_something.py - Good, but no challenge
- 04_why_fast.py - Fine, but passive
- 05-07 - Getting lecture-y
- 08-10 - Protein stuff feels like a leap

## What's missing?

1. No "try this" prompts
2. No puzzles or challenges
3. No "what happens if you break it?"
4. No self-assessment
5. Too much telling, not enough showing
6. No "show your friend" moment

## First instincts

- Each file should end with a CHALLENGE, not an explanation
- Remove all text that says "this is important because..."
- Add "# TRY THIS:" comments
- Add "# PUZZLE:" sections
- Make the discovery feel earned
- Less files, more depth per file
